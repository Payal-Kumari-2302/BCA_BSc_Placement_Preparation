# 🔴 PART 1 – NOUN

### Definition
A noun is a word that names a person, place, thing, animal, quality or idea.

## Important Noun Rules

### Rule 1: Types of Nouns

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Proper | Specific names | India, Rahul, Delhi |
| Common | General names | Boy, City, Teacher |
| Collective | Group names | Team, Family, Committee |
| Abstract | Qualities or ideas | Honesty, Beauty, Wisdom |
| Material | Names of substances | Gold, Silver, Iron |
| Concrete | Things perceived through senses | Book, Table, Apple |

### Rule 2: Uncountable Nouns

Uncountable nouns generally do not take plural forms.

**Structure:** Much / Little / Some + Uncountable Noun

| Incorrect ❌ | Correct ✅ |
|---|---|
| Informations | Information |
| Advices | Advice |
| Furnitures | Furniture |
| Equipments | Equipment |
| Luggages | Luggage |
| Knowledges | Knowledge |
| Sceneries | Scenery |
| Baggages | Baggage |

Examples:
- I need some information.
- She gave me good advice.

### Rule 3: Irregular Plural Nouns

Some nouns form their plurals by changing their spelling.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| Man | Men |
| Woman | Women |
| Child | Children |
| Foot | Feet |
| Tooth | Teeth |
| Mouse | Mice |
| Goose | Geese |
| Ox | Oxen |
| Person | People |

### Rule 4: Same Singular and Plural Forms

Some nouns have identical singular and plural forms.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| Sheep | Sheep |
| Deer | Deer |
| Fish | Fish |
| Aircraft | Aircraft |
| Species | Species |
| Series | Series |

### Rule 5: Nouns Taking Singular Verbs

Certain nouns ending in 's' are singular in meaning.

**Structure:** Singular Noun + Singular Verb

Examples:
- Mathematics is difficult.
- Physics is interesting.
- The news is good.
- Economics is an important subject.

### Rule 6: Nouns Taking Plural Verbs

Some nouns are generally used with plural verbs.

**Structure:** Plural Noun + Plural Verb

Examples:
- The police are investigating.
- The cattle are grazing.
- The scissors are sharp.
- My trousers are new.

### Rule 7: Each, Every, Either, Neither

These words generally take singular nouns and singular verbs.

**Structure:** Each / Every / Either / Neither + Singular Noun + Singular Verb

Examples:
- Each student is intelligent.
- Every employee has an identity card.
- Neither answer is correct.

### Rule 8: One of + Plural Noun

**Structure:** One of + The / My / These + Plural Noun + Singular Verb

Examples:
- One of my friends is a programmer.
- One of the students has passed.

### Rule 9: Much, Many, Few, Little

| Word | Used With | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Much | Uncountable | Much water |
| Many | Countable | Many students |
| Few | Countable | Few books |
| A few | Countable | A few people |
| Little | Uncountable | Little money |
| A little | Uncountable | A little milk |

### Rule 10: The Number of / A Number of

| Structure | Verb |
|---|---|
| The number of + Plural Noun | Singular |
| A number of + Plural Noun | Plural |

Examples:
- The number of students is increasing.
- A number of students are absent.

### Rule 11: Many a / More than one

Both expressions generally take singular nouns and singular verbs.

**Structures:**
- Many a + Singular Noun + Singular Verb
- More than one + Singular Noun + Singular Verb

Examples:
- Many a student has failed.
- More than one candidate is absent.

### Rule 12: Collective Nouns

A collective noun generally takes a singular verb when the group acts as one unit.

Examples:
- The team is playing well.
- The committee has taken a decision.
- The family lives together.

### Rule 13: Pair of Nouns

The verb agrees with the word 'pair' rather than the noun following it.

**Structure:** A / One Pair of + Plural Noun + Singular Verb

Examples:
- A pair of shoes is expensive.
- Two pairs of shoes are available.

### Rule 14: Possessive Nouns

Use apostrophes to indicate ownership.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| Singular + 's | Rahul's book |
| Plural ending in s + ' | Girls' hostel |
| Irregular plural + 's | Children's toys |

### Rule 15: Compound Nouns

In many compound nouns, the main word takes the plural form.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| Toothbrush | Toothbrushes |
| Classroom | Classrooms |
| Mother-in-law | Mothers-in-law |
| Passer-by | Passers-by |
| Editor-in-chief | Editors-in-chief |

### Rule 16: Number + Noun as an Adjective

A noun used as a compound adjective before another noun generally remains singular.

**Structure:** Number + Singular Noun + Noun

Examples:
- A five-year-old boy.
- A ten-day trip.
- A two-hour examination.

# 🔴 PART 2 – PRONOUN

### Definition
A pronoun is a word used in place of a noun to avoid repetition.

## Important Pronoun Rules

### Rule 1: Types of Pronouns

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Personal | I, We, You, He, She, They |
| Possessive | Mine, Ours, Yours, Hers, Theirs |
| Reflexive | Myself, Yourself, Himself |
| Demonstrative | This, That, These, Those |
| Relative | Who, Whom, Which, That |
| Interrogative | Who, What, Which, Whose |
| Indefinite | Someone, Anyone, Everybody |
| Distributive | Each, Either, Neither |
| Reciprocal | Each other, One another |

### Rule 2: Subjective Pronouns

Use subject pronouns as the subject of a sentence.

**Structure:** Subject Pronoun + Verb + Object

Examples:
- He is my brother.
- They are playing cricket.
- She works in an IT company.

### Rule 3: Objective Pronouns

Use object pronouns after verbs and prepositions.

**Structure:** Subject + Verb + Object Pronoun

Examples:
- She called me.
- The teacher helped him.
- Between you and me.

### Rule 4: Possessive Adjectives vs Possessive Pronouns

| Possessive Adjective | Possessive Pronoun |
|---|---|
| My | Mine |
| Our | Ours |
| Your | Yours |
| His | His |
| Her | Hers |
| Their | Theirs |

**Structures:**
- Possessive Adjective + Noun
- Noun + Be Verb + Possessive Pronoun

Examples:
- This is my book.
- This book is mine.

### Rule 5: Reflexive Pronouns

Use reflexive pronouns when the subject and object refer to the same person or thing.

**Structure:** Subject + Verb + Reflexive Pronoun

Examples:
- She hurt herself.
- I completed the work myself.
- They enjoyed themselves.

**Important:** Do not use myself as a replacement for I or me.

### Rule 6: Pronoun-Antecedent Agreement

A pronoun must agree with the noun it refers to in number and person.

Examples:
- The boy completed his homework.
- The girls completed their assignments.

### Rule 7: Who / Whom

| Pronoun | Usage |
|---|---|
| Who | Subject of a clause |
| Whom | Object of a verb or preposition |

Examples:
- Who called you?
- Whom did you meet?

### Rule 8: Whose / Who's

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Whose | Shows possession |
| Who's | Who is / Who has |

Examples:
- Whose bag is this?
- Who's coming today?

### Rule 9: Relative Pronouns

| Pronoun | Usage |
|---|---|
| Who | Persons as subjects |
| Whom | Persons as objects |
| Which | Animals and things |
| That | Persons, animals and things |
| Whose | Possession |

Example:
- The girl who won the prize is my sister.

### Rule 10: Either...or / Neither...nor

When the subjects differ in number, the verb generally agrees with the nearer subject.

**Structure:** Either / Neither + Subject 1 + Or / Nor + Subject 2 + Verb

Examples:
- Neither Ram nor his friends are present.
- Neither his friends nor Ram is present.

### Rule 11: One / One's

Maintain consistency when using 'one' as a pronoun.

**Structure:** One + Verb + One's + Noun

Example:
- One should respect one's parents.

### Rule 12: Let + Objective Pronoun

Use an objective pronoun after 'let'.

**Structure:** Let + Objective Pronoun + V1

Examples:
- Let him speak.
- Let her complete the work.

### Rule 13: Demonstrative Pronouns

| Pronoun | Usage |
|---|---|
| This | Singular and near |
| That | Singular and far |
| These | Plural and near |
| Those | Plural and far |

### Rule 14: Another / Other / Others

| Word | Usage |
|---|---|
| Another | One more |
| Other | Additional or different |
| Others | Other people or things |

Examples:
- Give me another chance.
- Other students are waiting.
- Some students passed while others failed.

### Rule 15: Reciprocal Pronouns

Use reciprocal pronouns to express a mutual relationship.

Examples:
- The two friends helped each other.
- The team members supported one another.

# 🔴 PART 3 – VERB

### Definition
A verb is a word that expresses an action, state or occurrence.

## Important Verb Rules

### Rule 1: Forms of Verbs

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | Base Form | Go |
| V2 | Past Form | Went |
| V3 | Past Participle | Gone |
| V4 | -ing Form | Going |
| V5 | s/es Form | Goes |

### Rule 2: Subject-Verb Agreement

A singular subject takes a singular verb, while a plural subject takes a plural verb.

**Structure:**
- Singular Subject + Singular Verb
- Plural Subject + Plural Verb

Examples:
- He works hard.
- They work hard.

### Rule 3: He, She, It + V5

In the simple present tense, third-person singular subjects generally take V5.

**Structure:** He / She / It + V5 + Object

Examples:
- She writes code.
- He plays football.
- It works properly.

### Rule 4: I, We, You, They + V1

In the simple present tense, these subjects generally take V1.

**Structure:** I / We / You / They + V1 + Object

Examples:
- They play cricket.
- We study English.

### Rule 5: Do / Does + V1

Use V1 after do and does in questions and negative sentences.

**Structures:**
- Do / Does + Subject + V1?
- Subject + Do / Does + Not + V1

Examples:
- Does she work here?
- He does not play cricket.

### Rule 6: Did + V1

Always use V1 after did.

**Structure:** Subject + Did + Not + V1

Examples:
- She did not attend the class.
- Did you complete the project?

### Rule 7: Has / Have / Had + V3

Use the past participle after perfect-tense helping verbs.

**Structures:**
- Has / Have + V3
- Had + V3

Examples:
- She has completed her work.
- They have submitted the assignment.
- He had left before I arrived.

### Rule 8: Is / Am / Are / Was / Were + V-ing

Use the -ing form for continuous actions.

**Structures:**
- Is / Am / Are + V-ing
- Was / Were + V-ing

Examples:
- She is studying.
- They are working.
- He was sleeping.

### Rule 9: Has / Have / Had Been + V-ing

Use this structure for perfect continuous tenses.

Examples:
- She has been studying for two hours.
- They had been waiting since morning.

### Rule 10: Passive Voice

Use the appropriate form of 'be' followed by V3 in passive constructions.

**Structure:** Subject + Be Verb + V3 + By + Agent

Examples:
- The work is completed by him.
- The letter was written by her.
- The project has been submitted.

### Rule 11: Modal + V1

Use the base form of the verb after modal auxiliaries.

**Structure:** Modal + V1

Examples:
- She can speak English.
- You should study regularly.
- He must complete the work.

### Rule 12: To + V1

Use the base form after the infinitive marker 'to'.

**Structure:** To + V1

Examples:
- I want to learn Python.
- She decided to apply for the job.
- He plans to study abroad.

### Rule 13: Gerund – Verb + ing

Use the -ing form as a noun after certain verbs and prepositions.

**Structure:** Verb / Preposition + V-ing

Examples:
- I enjoy reading.
- She avoids wasting time.
- He is interested in learning Python.

### Rule 14: Certain Verbs Followed by Gerunds

Some verbs are generally followed by V-ing.

**Structure:** Enjoy / Avoid / Finish / Mind / Suggest + V-ing

Examples:
- I enjoy coding.
- She finished writing.
- He suggested taking a break.

### Rule 15: Certain Verbs Followed by Infinitives

Some verbs are followed by to + V1.

**Structure:** Want / Decide / Hope / Plan / Promise + To + V1

Examples:
- I want to improve my English.
- She decided to join the company.
- He hopes to succeed.

### Rule 16: Make / Let + V1

Use V1 without 'to' after make and let in active constructions.

**Structure:** Make / Let + Object + V1

Examples:
- She made me laugh.
- Let him speak.

### Rule 17: Used To + V1

Use this structure for past habits or past situations.

**Structure:** Subject + Used To + V1

Example:
- I used to play cricket.

### Rule 18: Since / For with Perfect Tenses

Use since for a starting point and for for a duration.

| Since | For |
|---|---|
| Since Monday | For two days |
| Since 2020 | For six years |
| Since morning | For three hours |

Examples:
- I have lived here since 2020.
- She has worked here for two years.

### Rule 19: No Double Past Form

Do not use V2 after did.

❌ He did not went there.

✅ He did not go there.

### Rule 20: Subject Separated by Along With / As Well As

The verb agrees with the main subject, not the noun following these expressions.

**Structure:** Subject 1 + Along With / As Well As + Subject 2 + Verb

Examples:
- The manager along with his employees is attending the meeting.
- Rahul as well as his friends is present.

# 🔴 PART 4 – ADJECTIVE

### Definition
An adjective is a word that describes a noun or pronoun by expressing its quality, quantity, number or condition.

## Important Adjective Rules

### Rule 1: Types of Adjectives

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Quality | Beautiful, Intelligent, Honest |
| Quantity | Much, Little, Some |
| Number | Many, Few, Several |
| Demonstrative | This, That, These, Those |
| Possessive | My, Your, His, Their |
| Interrogative | Which, What, Whose |
| Distributive | Each, Every, Either, Neither |

### Rule 2: Adjective + Noun

**Structure:** Adjective + Noun

Examples:
- She is an intelligent girl.
- He bought a beautiful car.

### Rule 3: Linking Verb + Adjective

Use an adjective after linking verbs when describing the subject.

**Structure:** Subject + Linking Verb + Adjective

Examples:
- She looks beautiful.
- He feels happy.
- The food smells delicious.

### Rule 4: Adjective After Certain Verbs

Some verbs take adjectives to describe the object.

**Structure:** Make / Keep / Find / Consider + Object + Adjective

Examples:
- They made him angry.
- Keep your room clean.
- I found the question difficult.

### Rule 5: Adjective + Preposition

| Adjective | Preposition |
|---|---|
| Good | At |
| Interested | In |
| Afraid | Of |
| Famous | For |
| Proud | Of |
| Different | From |
| Senior | To |
| Junior | To |
| Responsible | For |
| Capable | Of |
| Similar | To |
| Married | To |
| Superior | To |
| Inferior | To |

### Rule 6: Degrees of Comparison

| Degree | Example |
|---|---|
| Positive | Tall |
| Comparative | Taller |
| Superlative | Tallest |

### Rule 7: Comparative Degree

Use the comparative form to compare two people or things.

**Structure:** Subject + Comparative Adjective + Than

Example:
- Rahul is taller than Aman.

### Rule 8: Superlative Degree

Use the superlative form to compare one person or thing with a group.

**Structure:** Subject + The + Superlative Adjective

Example:
- Rahul is the tallest boy in the class.

### Rule 9: No Double Comparison

Do not use more and -er together or most and -est together.

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| More smarter | Smarter |
| More easier | Easier |
| Most tallest | Tallest |
| Most happiest | Happiest |

### Rule 10: As + Adjective + As

Use the positive degree to show equality.

**Structure:** As + Adjective + As

Example:
- She is as intelligent as her sister.

### Rule 11: Too + Adjective + To

**Structure:** Too + Adjective + To + V1

Example:
- He is too weak to walk.

### Rule 12: So + Adjective + That

**Structure:** So + Adjective + That + Clause

Example:
- He was so tired that he slept immediately.

### Rule 13: Such + A / An + Adjective + Noun

**Structure:** Such + A / An + Adjective + Singular Countable Noun

Example:
- She is such an intelligent girl.

### Rule 14: Adjective + Enough

Enough comes after an adjective.

**Structure:** Adjective + Enough + To + V1

Example:
- He is strong enough to lift the box.

### Rule 15: Enough + Noun

Enough comes before a noun.

**Structure:** Enough + Noun

Example:
- We have enough money.

### Rule 16: Adjective Order

When multiple adjectives describe one noun, generally follow this order:

**Opinion → Size → Age → Shape → Colour → Origin → Material → Purpose → Noun**

Example:
- She bought a beautiful small new red Italian leather bag.

### Rule 17: Less / Fewer

| Word | Usage |
|---|---|
| Less | Uncountable quantities |
| Fewer | Countable items |

Examples:
- We have less water.
- There are fewer students.

### Rule 18: Elder / Older

- Elder is generally used for family relationships.
- Older is used for general age comparisons.

Examples:
- My elder brother is a doctor.
- Rahul is older than Aman.

### Rule 19: One of the + Superlative

**Structure:** One of the + The + Superlative Adjective + Plural Noun

Example:
- She is one of the most intelligent students in the class.

### Rule 20: Comparative of Two

Use the comparative degree when comparing two people or things.

Example:
- This is the better of the two options.

# 🔴 PART 5 – ADVERB

### Definition
An adverb is a word that modifies a verb, adjective, another adverb or an entire sentence.

## Important Adverb Rules

### Rule 1: Types of Adverbs

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Manner | Slowly, Carefully, Quickly |
| Time | Today, Yesterday, Now |
| Place | Here, There, Everywhere |
| Frequency | Always, Often, Never |
| Degree | Very, Too, Quite |
| Reason | Therefore, Hence |
| Affirmation | Certainly, Surely |
| Negation | Not, Never |

### Rule 2: Verb + Adverb

Adverbs of manner describe how an action happens.

**Structure:** Verb + Adverb

Examples:
- She speaks politely.
- He works carefully.
- They completed the task quickly.

### Rule 3: Adjective + Adverb of Degree

Adverbs of degree modify adjectives.

**Structure:** Adverb of Degree + Adjective

Examples:
- She is very intelligent.
- He is extremely talented.
- The question is quite difficult.

### Rule 4: Adverb + Adverb

An adverb can modify another adverb.

**Structure:** Adverb + Adverb + Verb

Example:
- She runs very quickly.

### Rule 5: Adjective vs Adverb

Use an adjective to describe a noun or pronoun and an adverb to modify a verb.

| Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|
| Quick | Quickly |
| Slow | Slowly |
| Careful | Carefully |
| Beautiful | Beautifully |
| Honest | Honestly |

Examples:
- She is a careful driver.
- She drives carefully.

### Rule 6: Good vs Well

- Good = Adjective.
- Well = Adverb in its usual manner-related use.

Examples:
- She is a good student.
- She speaks English well.

### Rule 7: Hard vs Hardly

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hard | With effort |
| Hardly | Almost not |

Examples:
- He works hard.
- He hardly works.

### Rule 8: Late vs Lately

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Late | Not on time |
| Lately | Recently |

Examples:
- He arrived late.
- I have been busy lately.

### Rule 9: Enough Position

Enough comes after an adjective or adverb.

**Structure:** Adjective / Adverb + Enough + To + V1

Examples:
- She is intelligent enough to solve it.
- He runs fast enough to win.

### Rule 10: Too + Adverb

**Structure:** Too + Adverb + To + V1

Example:
- He speaks too quickly to understand.

### Rule 11: Frequency Adverbs

Adverbs of frequency generally come before the main verb but after the verb 'be'.

**Structures:**
- Subject + Frequency Adverb + Main Verb
- Subject + Be Verb + Frequency Adverb

Examples:
- She always studies.
- He is always punctual.

### Rule 12: Comparative Adverbs

Use comparative adverbs to compare two actions.

**Structure:** More / Less + Adverb + Than

Examples:
- She speaks more clearly than him.
- He works harder than his brother.

### Rule 13: Superlative Adverbs

Use the superlative form to compare more than two actions.

Examples:
- She runs the fastest.
- He works the most efficiently.

### Rule 14: Too vs Very

- Very expresses a high degree.
- Too expresses an excessive degree.

Examples:
- The question is very difficult.
- The question is too difficult to solve.

### Rule 15: No Double Negative

Avoid using two negative expressions in the same clause when they cancel each other.

❌ I don't know nothing.

✅ I don't know anything.

### Rule 16: Enough / Too / So

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| Very + Adverb | She speaks very clearly. |
| Too + Adverb + To | He runs too slowly to win. |
| Adverb + Enough | She speaks clearly enough. |
| So + Adverb + That | He ran so quickly that nobody caught him. |

# 🔴 PART 6 – PREPOSITION

### Definition
A preposition is a word that shows the relationship of a noun or pronoun with another word in a sentence.

## Important Preposition Rules

### Rule 1: Prepositions of Time

| Preposition | Usage | Example |
|---|---|---|
| At | Exact time | At 7 AM |
| On | Days and dates | On Monday |
| In | Months, years, periods | In 2026 |
| Since | Starting point | Since 2020 |
| For | Duration | For two years |
| During | Period of time | During the meeting |
| By | Deadline | By Monday |
| Until | Up to a time | Until evening |

### Rule 2: Prepositions of Place

| Preposition | Usage |
|---|---|
| In | Inside something |
| On | On a surface |
| At | Specific location |
| Under | Below something |
| Above | Higher than |
| Between | Separating two |
| Among | Within a group |
| Behind | At the back |
| Beside | Next to |
| Near | Close to |

### Rule 3: Prepositions of Movement

| Preposition | Usage |
|---|---|
| To | Destination |
| Into | Movement inside |
| Onto | Movement to a surface |
| Through | From one side to another |
| Across | From one side to the other |
| Along | Following a path |
| Towards | In the direction of |
| From | Starting point |

### Rule 4: Preposition + V-ing

Use the gerund form after a preposition.

**Structure:** Preposition + V-ing

Examples:
- She is interested in learning.
- He left without saying anything.
- I am good at solving problems.

### Rule 5: Between / Among

- Between is generally used for two or distinct individual items.
- Among is generally used for a group.

Examples:
- Divide the money between Ram and Shyam.
- She was sitting among her friends.

### Rule 6: By / With

| Preposition | Usage |
|---|---|
| By | Agent or method |
| With | Instrument or tool |

Examples:
- The letter was written by Rahul.
- He cut the paper with scissors.

### Rule 7: Since / For

| Since | For |
|---|---|
| Since Monday | For two days |
| Since 2020 | For six years |
| Since morning | For three hours |

### Rule 8: Beside / Besides

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Beside | Next to |
| Besides | In addition to |

Examples:
- Sit beside me.
- Besides English, she knows Hindi.

### Rule 9: In / Into

| Word | Usage |
|---|---|
| In | Position |
| Into | Movement inside |

Examples:
- She is in the room.
- She went into the room.

### Rule 10: On / Upon

- On generally indicates position.
- Upon can indicate movement or a more formal sense of position, depending on context.

Examples:
- The book is on the table.
- Once upon a time.

### Rule 11: Common Preposition Combinations

| Expression | Correct Preposition |
|---|---|
| Depend | On |
| Belong | To |
| Listen | To |
| Wait | For |
| Believe | In |
| Apologize | For |
| Consist | Of |
| Suffer | From |
| Apply | For |
| Agree | With |
| Insist | On |
| Look | At |
| Search | For |
| Deal | With |

### Rule 12: No Unnecessary Preposition

Some verbs do not require an additional preposition.

| Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|
| Discuss about the topic | Discuss the topic |
| Enter into the room | Enter the room |
| Return back | Return |
| Order for food | Order food |
| Reach to the station | Reach the station |

# 🔴 PART 7 – CONJUNCTION

### Definition
A conjunction is a word that connects words, phrases or clauses.

## Important Conjunction Rules

### Rule 1: Types of Conjunctions

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Coordinating | And, But, Or, Nor, Yet, So |
| Subordinating | Because, Although, Since, Unless, While |
| Correlative | Either...or, Neither...nor, Both...and |

### Rule 2: And

Use 'and' to connect similar ideas.

**Structure:** Subject / Word + And + Subject / Word

Example:
- Rahul and Aman are friends.

### Rule 3: But

Use 'but' to show contrast.

**Structure:** Clause + But + Contrasting Clause

Example:
- She is intelligent but careless.

### Rule 4: Or

Use 'or' to show alternatives.

**Structure:** Option 1 + Or + Option 2

Example:
- You can take tea or coffee.

### Rule 5: Because / Because of

| Word | Structure |
|---|---|
| Because | Because + Subject + Verb |
| Because of | Because of + Noun / Pronoun / V-ing |

Examples:
- He failed because he was careless.
- He failed because of his carelessness.

### Rule 6: Although / Despite

| Word | Structure |
|---|---|
| Although | Although + Subject + Verb |
| Despite | Despite + Noun / Pronoun / V-ing |
| In spite of | In spite of + Noun / Pronoun / V-ing |

Examples:
- Although he was tired, he worked.
- Despite being tired, he worked.

### Rule 7: Unless

Unless means 'if not'.

**Structure:** Unless + Present Simple, Main Clause

Example:
- Unless you study, you will fail.

### Rule 8: Either...or

Use either...or to express two alternatives.

**Structure:** Either + Option 1 + Or + Option 2

Example:
- Either Rahul or Aman will attend.

### Rule 9: Neither...nor

Use neither...nor to express two negative alternatives.

**Structure:** Neither + Option 1 + Nor + Option 2

Example:
- Neither Rahul nor Aman is present.

### Rule 10: Both...and

Use both...and to include two things or people.

**Structure:** Both + Option 1 + And + Option 2

Example:
- Both Ram and Shyam are intelligent.

### Rule 11: Not Only...But Also

Use this structure to emphasize two related points.

**Structure:** Not Only + First Part + But Also + Second Part

Example:
- She is not only intelligent but also hardworking.

### Rule 12: Whether...or

Use whether...or to express alternatives or uncertainty.

Example:
- I don't know whether he will come or not.

### Rule 13: So...that

Use so...that to express a result.

**Structure:** So + Adjective / Adverb + That + Clause

Example:
- He was so tired that he slept immediately.

### Rule 14: Such...that

Use such...that to express a result involving a noun phrase.

**Structure:** Such + A / An + Adjective + Noun + That + Clause

Example:
- It was such a difficult question that nobody could solve it.

### Rule 15: No Double Conjunction

Avoid using although and but together for the same contrast.

❌ Although he was poor, but he was honest.

✅ Although he was poor, he was honest.

### Rule 16: No Double Reason

Avoid using because and so together to express the same cause-result relationship.

❌ Because he was ill, so he stayed home.

✅ Because he was ill, he stayed home.

# 🔴 PART 8 – INTERJECTION

### Definition
An interjection is a word or expression used to express sudden emotions or feelings.

## Important Interjection Rules

### Rule 1: Types of Interjections

| Emotion | Interjection | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Joy | Hurrah! | Hurrah! We won the match. |
| Sadness | Alas! | Alas! He lost his job. |
| Surprise | Wow! | Wow! What a beautiful view! |
| Pain | Ouch! | Ouch! That hurts. |
| Greeting | Hello! | Hello! How are you? |
| Approval | Bravo! | Bravo! You did well. |
| Attention | Hey! | Hey! Listen to me. |
| Disgust | Ugh! | Ugh! This smells terrible. |

### Rule 2: Interjection + Exclamation Mark

Use an exclamation mark to express strong emotions.

**Structure:** Interjection + !

Example:
- Hurrah! We have won.

### Rule 3: Interjection at the Beginning

An interjection generally appears at the beginning of a sentence.

Examples:
- Wow! That is amazing.
- Alas! We lost the match.

### Rule 4: Interjection with a Comma

Use a comma when the emotion is mild.

Examples:
- Well, I agree with you.
- Oh, I understand now.
