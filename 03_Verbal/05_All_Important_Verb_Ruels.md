# PART 1: IMPORTANT VERB RULES

## 🔴 PRIORITY 1 – Must Learn

### Rule 1: Five Forms of Verbs

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | Base Form | Go |
| V2 | Past Form | Went |
| V3 | Past Participle | Gone |
| V4 | -ing Form | Going |
| V5 | s/es Form | Goes |

### Rule 2: Do / Does / Did + V1
- Do/Does/Did are followed by the base form of the verb.

**Examples:**
- She does not like coffee.
- He did not attend the meeting.

### Rule 3: Has / Have / Had + V3
- Has/Have/Had are followed by the past participle (V3).

**Examples:**
- She has completed her work.
- They have gone home.
- He had finished the task.

### Rule 4: Is / Am / Are + V4
- Used to form the present continuous tense.

**Examples:**
- She is studying.
- They are playing.

### Rule 5: Was / Were + V4
- Used to form the past continuous tense.

**Examples:**
- He was sleeping.
- They were working.

### Rule 6: Be Verb + V3
- Used to form passive voice.

**Examples:**
- The work is completed.
- The letter was written by him.

### Rule 7: To + V1
- The infinitive marker "to" is generally followed by V1.

**Examples:**
- I want to learn Python.
- She decided to leave.

### Rule 8: Preposition + V4
- A verb following a preposition generally takes the -ing form.

**Examples:**
- She is interested in learning.
- He left without informing me.

### Rule 9: Certain Verbs + V4

The following verbs are commonly followed by V4:

**Enjoy, Avoid, Finish, Mind, Suggest, Practice, Admit, Deny**

**Examples:**
- I enjoy reading.
- Avoid making mistakes.
- She suggested going there.

### Rule 10: Certain Verbs + To + V1

The following verbs are commonly followed by an infinitive:

**Want, Decide, Hope, Promise, Agree, Plan, Refuse, Manage**

**Examples:**
- I want to improve.
- She decided to study.
- He refused to help.

### Rule 11: Let + Object + V1

**Structure:** Let + Object + V1

**Examples:**
- Let me speak.
- She let him go.

### Rule 12: Make + Object + V1

**Structure:** Make + Object + V1

**Examples:**
- The teacher made us write.
- They made him apologize.

## 🟡 PRIORITY 2 – Important for Error Spotting

### Rule 13: Help + Object + V1 / To + V1

Both structures are correct.

**Examples:**
- She helped me complete the task.
- She helped me to complete the task.

### Rule 14: Look Forward To + V4

Here, "to" is a preposition, so it is followed by V4.

**Examples:**
- I look forward to meeting you.
- She looks forward to joining the company.

### Rule 15: Prefer + V4 / To + V1

**Examples:**
- I prefer reading to watching TV.
- I prefer to work alone.

### Rule 16: Stop + V4 / To + V1

The meaning changes according to the verb form.

| Structure | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Stop + V4 | Stop an activity | He stopped smoking. |
| Stop + To + V1 | Stop another activity to do something | He stopped to drink water. |

### Rule 17: Remember + V4 / To + V1

| Structure | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Remember + V4 | Remember a past action | I remember meeting him. |
| Remember + To + V1 | Remember to do something | Remember to call me. |

### Rule 18: Forget + V4 / To + V1

| Structure | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Forget + V4 | Forget a past experience | I will never forget meeting her. |
| Forget + To + V1 | Fail to remember something that must be done | He forgot to call me. |

### Rule 19: See / Hear / Watch + Object + V1 / V4

- V1: Complete action.
- V4: Action in progress.

**Examples:**
- I saw him cross the road.
- I saw him crossing the road.

### Rule 20: Make in Passive Voice

- Active: Make + Object + V1
- Passive: Be Made + To + V1

**Examples:**
- Active: They made him work.
- Passive: He was made to work.

### Rule 21: Regular and Irregular Verbs

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Regular | V2 and V3 usually end in -ed | Work – Worked – Worked |
| Irregular | V2 and V3 do not follow the regular -ed pattern | Go – Went – Gone |

### Rule 22: Transitive Verbs

- A verb that requires an object to complete its meaning.

**Example:**
- She bought a book.
- He opened the door.

### Rule 23: Intransitive Verbs

- A verb that does not require an object.

**Example:**
- The baby cried.
- Birds fly.

## 🟢 PRIORITY 3 – Conceptual Understanding

### Rule 24: Linking Verbs

- Linking verbs connect the subject with its description or identity.

**Common Linking Verbs:** Is, Am, Are, Was, Were, Become, Seem, Feel, Look, Appear.

**Examples:**
- She is intelligent.
- He became a doctor.

### Rule 25: Stative Verbs

- Stative verbs describe a state, feeling, possession, or thought rather than an action.
- They are generally not used in continuous forms when expressing a state.

**Common Verbs:** Know, Believe, Understand, Love, Hate, Own, Need, Prefer.

**Examples:**
- I know the answer.
- She owns a car.

### Rule 26: Causative Verbs

- Causative verbs show that someone causes another person to do something.

**Common Verbs:** Make, Have, Get, Let.

**Examples:**
- She made me laugh.
- I had my car repaired.
- He got his work completed.

### Rule 27: Ditransitive Verbs

- A verb that takes two objects: a direct object and an indirect object.

**Examples:**
- She gave me a book.
- He sent his friend a message.

### Rule 28: Bare Infinitive

- A bare infinitive is the base form of a verb without "to".

**Examples:**
- Let him go.
- She can dance.
- I saw him run.
