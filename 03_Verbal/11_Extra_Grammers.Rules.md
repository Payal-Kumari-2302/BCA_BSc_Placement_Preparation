# ADDITIONAL GRAMMAR – Important Rules & Error Spotting

**Target Companies:** TCS, TCS Ignite, TCS NQT, Wipro, Infosys, Accenture, Cognizant, Deloitte, Genpact & Other IT Companies.

**Exam Priority:** 🔴 High | 🟡 Medium

## 1. PARTICIPLES
**Priority: 🔴 High**

**Definition:** A participle is a verb form used as an adjective or to form verb tenses.

| Rule | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present Participle | V1 + ing | The crying baby needs attention. |
| Past Participle | V3 | The broken glass was on the floor. |
| Perfect Participle | Having + V3 | Having completed the work, she went home. |
| Perfect Passive Participle | Having been + V3 | Having been warned, he remained careful. |

**Rule 5:** A participial phrase must logically describe the subject.

Incorrect: Walking to school, the rain started.  
Correct: Walking to school, I got caught in the rain.

## 2. PARALLELISM / PARALLEL STRUCTURE
**Priority: 🔴 High**

**Definition:** Parallelism means using the same grammatical form for items in a series or comparison.

| Rule | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Series | V-ing + V-ing + V-ing | She enjoys reading, writing and dancing. |
| Both...And | Both + X + and + X | She is both intelligent and hardworking. |
| Either...Or | Either + X + or + X | You can either study or work. |
| Neither...Nor | Neither + X + nor + X | He neither sings nor dances. |
| Not Only...But Also | Not only + X + but also + X | She is not only intelligent but also hardworking. |
| Comparison | Same grammatical form | I prefer reading to watching TV. |

## 3. PRONOUN-ANTECEDENT AGREEMENT
**Priority: 🔴 High**

**Definition:** A pronoun must agree with its antecedent in number and person.

| Rule | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Singular Antecedent | Singular Noun + Singular Pronoun | Every student must bring his or her ID. |
| Plural Antecedent | Plural Noun + Plural Pronoun | Students submitted their assignments. |
| Each / Every | Singular Pronoun | Each student must do his or her work. |
| Either / Neither | Singular Pronoun | Neither boy forgot his bag. |
| Two Subjects with And | Plural Pronoun | Ram and Shyam completed their work. |

**Rule 6:** A pronoun must clearly refer to its intended noun.

Incorrect: When Rahul met Amit, he was angry.  
Correct: Rahul was angry when he met Amit.

## 4. MODIFIERS
**Priority: 🔴 High**

**Definition:** A modifier is a word or phrase that describes another word or provides additional information.

| Rule | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Modifier Placement | Modifier + Related Word | She bought a beautiful dress. |
| Misplaced Modifier | Place modifier near the word described | She almost drove for six hours. |
| Dangling Modifier | Modifier must describe the subject | Walking home, I saw a dog. |
| Only Placement | Only + Word Being Emphasized | Only Rahul passed the exam. |
| Adjective Placement | Adjective + Noun | She is an intelligent student. |

**Important Rule:** Incorrect modifier placement can change the meaning of a sentence.

Incorrect: After finishing the exam, the bell rang.  
Correct: After finishing the exam, I heard the bell ring.

## 5. RELATIVE PRONOUNS & RELATIVE CLAUSES
**Priority: 🔴 High**

**Definition:** Relative pronouns introduce clauses that describe nouns or pronouns.

| Rule | Usage | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Who | People as Subject | The girl who won is my sister. |
| Whom | People as Object | The man whom I met was a doctor. |
| Whose | Possession | The student whose book was lost is absent. |
| Which | Animals and Things | This is the book which I purchased. |
| That | People, Animals and Things in Defining Clauses | The book that I bought is interesting. |

**Rule 6:** Defining clauses do not require commas.  
Example: The student who works hard will succeed.

**Rule 7:** Non-defining clauses use commas; generally, THAT is not used.  
Example: Rahul, who lives in Delhi, is my friend.

## 6. SENTENCE STRUCTURE
**Priority: 🔴 High**

**Definition:** Sentence structure refers to the arrangement of clauses in a sentence.

| Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Simple | One Independent Clause | She studies every day. |
| Compound | Independent Clause + FANBOYS + Independent Clause | I was tired, but I continued working. |
| Complex | Independent Clause + Dependent Clause | I stayed home because it was raining. |
| Compound-Complex | 2+ Independent Clauses + 1+ Dependent Clause | I studied because the exam was near, and I passed. |

**FANBOYS:** For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So.

**Independent Clause:** Expresses a complete thought.  
**Dependent Clause:** Cannot stand alone as a complete sentence.

## 7. SENTENCE FRAGMENTS & RUN-ON SENTENCES
**Priority: 🟡 Medium**

**Definition:** A sentence fragment is an incomplete sentence, whereas a run-on sentence incorrectly joins independent clauses.

| Rule | Incorrect | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| Incomplete Sentence | Because he was tired. | He slept because he was tired. |
| Missing Subject | Went to the market. | She went to the market. |
| Missing Verb | The students in the classroom. | The students are in the classroom. |
| Run-on Sentence | I was tired I went home. | I was tired, so I went home. |
| Comma Splice | She is intelligent, she is hardworking. | She is intelligent, and she is hardworking. |

**Important Rule:** Two independent clauses must be joined using a suitable conjunction, semicolon or full stop.

## 8. COMMONLY CONFUSED WORDS
**Priority: 🔴 High**

**Definition:** Commonly confused words have similar spellings, pronunciations or meanings but different grammatical uses.

| Word 1 | Word 2 | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| Accept | Except | Receive / Exclude |
| Affect | Effect | Influence / Result |
| Advice | Advise | Noun / Verb |
| Principal | Principle | Head / Rule |
| Complement | Compliment | Complete / Praise |
| Stationary | Stationery | Not moving / Writing materials |
| Lose | Loose | Fail to keep / Not tight |
| Than | Then | Comparison / Time |
| Its | It's | Possessive / It is |
| Your | You're | Possessive / You are |
| Their | There | Possessive / Place |
| Beside | Besides | Next to / In addition to |
| Ensure | Insure | Make certain / Provide insurance |
| Fewer | Less | Countable / Uncountable |
| Farther | Further | Physical distance / Additional extent |

**Important Rules:**
1. Fewer + Countable Nouns; Less + Uncountable Nouns.
2. Many + Countable Nouns; Much + Uncountable Nouns.
3. Some is commonly used in affirmative sentences; Any is commonly used in negative sentences and questions.

## 9. WORD ORDER
**Priority: 🟡 Medium**

**Definition:** Word order refers to the correct arrangement of words in a sentence.

| Rule | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Basic Sentence | Subject + Verb + Object | She reads books. |
| Adjective Placement | Adjective + Noun | She bought a beautiful dress. |
| Adverb of Frequency | Before Main Verb | She always arrives early. |
| With Be Verb | Be + Adverb | She is always punctual. |
| Indirect Questions | Question Word + Subject + Verb | I don't know where he lives. |
| Adverb of Manner | Verb/Object + Adverb | She speaks English fluently. |
| Time and Place | Place + Time | She met me at the office yesterday. |

## 10. PUNCTUATION RULES
**Priority: 🟡 Medium**

**Definition:** Punctuation marks help organize sentences and clarify their meaning.

| Punctuation | Usage | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Full Stop (.) | End of a statement | She is a student. |
| Comma (,) | Separate items or clauses | I bought apples, bananas and oranges. |
| Apostrophe (') | Possession or contraction | Rahul's book. / Don't go. |
| Semicolon (;) | Connect related independent clauses | I was tired; I continued working. |
| Colon (:) | Introduce a list or explanation | I need three things: a pen, a book and a bag. |
| Question Mark (?) | Direct questions | Where do you live? |
| Quotation Marks (" ") | Direct speech | She said, "I am ready." |

**Capitalization Rules:**
1. Capitalize the first word of a sentence.
2. Capitalize proper nouns.
3. Capitalize days and months.
4. Always capitalize the pronoun I.
