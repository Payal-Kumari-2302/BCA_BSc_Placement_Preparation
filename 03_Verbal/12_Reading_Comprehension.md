# SET 1 – Technology & Automation

### Passage

In recent years, technology has significantly changed the way businesses operate. Companies increasingly rely on automation to improve efficiency, reduce operational costs and complete repetitive tasks faster. However, the growing use of automation has also raised concerns about employment opportunities.

While some people believe that automation will eliminate a large number of jobs, others argue that it will create new opportunities requiring different skill sets. Historically, technological advancements have not simply destroyed employment; they have transformed the nature of work. For instance, the introduction of computers reduced the demand for certain manual activities but increased the need for software developers, data analysts and IT professionals.

The real challenge, therefore, is not automation itself but the ability of workers to adapt to changing requirements. Employees who continuously upgrade their skills are more likely to remain relevant in the job market. Educational institutions and companies also have an important role in preparing individuals for these changes through practical training and skill development.

Ultimately, technological progress can benefit society, but its advantages depend on how effectively people and organisations respond to it.

# SET 2 – Skills & Employment

### Passage

In today's competitive job market, possessing a degree is no longer sufficient to guarantee employment. Employers increasingly seek candidates who can apply theoretical knowledge to practical situations. While academic qualifications demonstrate a person's understanding of fundamental concepts, practical skills reveal their ability to solve real-world problems.

Many graduates struggle to secure employment because they lack industry exposure and practical experience. To address this gap, companies are increasingly focusing on internships, project-based assessments and skill-based hiring. These methods help employers evaluate candidates beyond their academic performance.

However, practical knowledge alone is not enough. Communication, teamwork and adaptability are equally important because modern workplaces require individuals to collaborate and respond effectively to changing circumstances.

Therefore, students should focus on developing a balanced combination of technical knowledge, practical experience and interpersonal skills to improve their career prospects.

# The Paradox of Technological Progress

Technological advancement is often regarded as an unquestionable indicator of societal progress. Innovations in communication, transportation and healthcare have undoubtedly improved the quality of human life. However, the assumption that every technological development necessarily benefits society deserves closer examination.

One significant consequence of technological progress is the growing dependence on automated systems. Although automation increases productivity and minimises human error in repetitive tasks, it can also weaken certain skills when individuals rely excessively on technology. For instance, navigation applications have made travelling more convenient, but constant dependence on them may reduce people's ability to interpret maps or remember routes independently.

Furthermore, technological benefits are not distributed equally. While developed economies often possess the infrastructure and resources necessary to adopt advanced technologies, less developed regions may struggle to access them. This disparity can widen existing economic inequalities rather than eliminate them.

Nevertheless, rejecting technological advancement is neither practical nor desirable. The real objective should be to ensure that innovation complements human capabilities instead of replacing independent thinking. Societies must therefore balance technological efficiency with education, accessibility and responsible usage.
